What Is Anxiety

Anxiety is a completely normal feeling. It alerts us to danger and prepares us to react. It is the body's natural alarm system. The anxiety is experienced as threatening, but the danger is often not real. It prevents you from doing the things you want and need to do. An anxiety disorder is not a sign of weakness. It is a mental illness with clear causes and effective treatment.

Around 20–25% of the population will experience an anxiety disorder during their lifetime, according to Helsedirektoratet. At any given time, around 15% of Norwegians meet the diagnostic criteria for an anxiety disorder. The term "anxiety" covers many different conditions. The common feelings are fear and worry that are difficult to control, and they negatively affect your everyday life.

Anxiety vs. Stress

Stress Stress is usually related to a specific event and goes away when it is over
Anxiety Anxiety is persistent and can occur for no apparent reason.

The combination of symptoms of anxiety and stress is very common and should be taken seriously.

Symptoms Of Anxiety

Anxiety causes both physical and psychological symptoms. Anxiety shows up in your body, your thoughts, and your behavior. The body reacts as if there is danger even when there is not. Here are the most common signs.

Anxiety Persistent worry, fear, and restlessness that get in the way of daily life. The most common mental illness.
Depression Persistent low mood, loss of interest, fatigue, and negative thinking. Often people experience mild to severe episodes of depression.
Stress & burnout Work stress, chronic pressure and exhaustion syndrome. One of the most common reasons for sick leave at the workplace.
Sleep problems Insomnia and sleep difficulties affect around 1 in 7 adults and have a direct impact on mental health.
ADHD Attention, impulse, and executive function challenges that persist into adulthood for most people diagnosed as children.
Bipolar disorder Episodes of mania and depression alternating over time. A serious but manageable condition with mood stabilisers and therapy.
Personality disorders Persistent patterns in how you think, feel and relate to others that cause distress. Includes borderline, avoidant and narcissistic personality disorder.
Eating disorders Anorexia, bulimia and binge eating disorder. Serious conditions with significant physical and psychological impact.
Grief & loss Normal grief becomes complicated grief when it does not ease over time and prevents you from functioning. Professional support is beneficial.

Diagnostic Criteria For
Anxiety Disorders(ICD-10)

In Norway, the diagnosis is usually made based on the criteria in ICD-10, the diagnostic system used in the Norwegian healthcare system. These are the core criteria a psychologist or doctor checks during an assessment. Diagnostic criteria according to ICD-10.

  • Persistent worry that is difficult to control
  • Restlessness or feeling of being on your toes
  • Easy fatigue and lack of energy
  • Difficulty concentrating, with an empty mind
  • Irritability and mood swings
  • Muscle tension and headaches
  • Sleep disturbances (difficulty falling asleep/staying asleep)
  • Avoidance behavior

Panic Attack

A panic attack is a sudden wave of intense fear. It can feel like a heartattack and be very uncomfortable.

Most people with panic disorder show improvement with the right treatment. Cognitive behavioural therapy with exposure training is very effective. A psychologist helps you understand what is happening in your body. Moreover, it teaches you to face your anxiety instead of running away from it. However, if you experience these symptoms for the first time or are unsure of the cause, seek medical attention to rule out other conditions.

Who Is Most At Risk Of Anxiety?

But some factors make it more likely. Knowing these risk factors does not mean anxiety is reversible; it means you can be more aware and get professional support.

Family history

If a parent or close relative has an anxiety disorder, your risk is higher. This is genetic and learned behavior. Moreover, watching a parent respond to the world with anxiety can teach that pattern.

Difficult childhood

Abuse, neglect, bullying, instability or early loss all raise the risk of anxiety disorders in adulthood. The brain learns early on whether the world is a safe place.

Other mental health conditions

Depression, ADHD, personality disorders and eating disorders all increase the risk of anxiety disorders. They often co occur and reinforce each other.

Chronic stress

Long term stress at work, in relationships, or with finances. The body can only sustain high alert mode for so long before anxiety sets in as a default state.

Substance use

Alcohol and drugs are often used to manage anxiety in the short term, but they make it worse over time. Withdrawal from alcohol can cause intense anxiety.

Treatment Of Anxiety Disorders

Anxiety is very well treated. Cognitive therapy for anxiety is the effective treatment, and for many, it is as effective as medication. The right treatment depends on the type of anxiety and how severe it is.

Cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT)

Teaches you to identify thought patterns that create anxiety and replace them with more realistic thoughts. Combined with gradual exposure to what you fear.

Exposure therapy

You gradually approach what you fear and discover that it is not dangerous. Especially effective for phobias, OCD, social anxiety, and panic disorder.

Mindfulness and ACT

Acceptance and Commitment Therapy teaches you to observe your anxiety without fighting it. It reduces relapses and is especially helpful for generalized anxiety.

Anxiety medication

Medication combined with therapy gives the best results. Anxiety medication should always be taken after consulting a doctor.

Psychoeducation about anxiety

Knowledge is power. Understanding what anxiety is, what happens in the body, and what keeps it going is an important first step in treatment.

Digital tools

Mental health apps can help support treatment between sessions. They are not a replacement for therapy.

When Should You Seek Help For Your Mental Health?

If mental health symptoms are affecting your daily life, relationships, work, or overall well-being, it may be time to seek professional support. You do not need to have a diagnosis to talk to a psychologist.

We connect you with experienced psychologists across Norway, without a referral, and with no long waiting list. Early support can help you understand what you are experiencing and explore appropriate ways to manage it.
