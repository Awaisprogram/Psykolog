Mental Health

Mental health is about how you feel about yourself. How you cope with everyday challenges and how you function in relationships and work. Good mental health is not the absence of difficult emotions. However, it is the ability to manage them. Approximately 1 in 2 Norwegians will experience a mental illness during their lifetime.

What is Mental Health?

Mental health is not just the absence of mental illness. It is the overall state of your psychological, emotional, and social well-being. Everyone has mental health, in the same way that everyone has physical health. It can be good or poor. And it can change throughout life.

Mental health is a state of well-being in which an individual realizes their own abilities, can cope with the normal stresses of life, and can work productively. Good mental health does not mean feeling happy all the time. It means having the tools to manage difficult feelings without those feelings taking over your life.

In Norway, mental health is best understood through the biopsychosocial model. This means that the interaction between three things shapes your mental health.

The biopsychosocial model

Biological Genetics, brain chemistry, and hormones
Psychological Thoughts, emotions, behaviors, and coping
Social Relationships, culture, socioeconomic factors

Connection Between Physical Activity And Mental Health

The connection between physical and mental health is not a cliché; it is biology. When you exercise, your brain releases dopamine, serotonin, and endorphins. The same chemicals that many psychiatric medications try to boost. Building regular movement into your life is one of the highest-impact changes you can make for your mental health. Alongside sleep, social connection and professional support when needed.

WHAT IMPROVES HOW IT HELPS
Brain chemistry
Exercise boosts serotonin, dopamine, and norepinephrine, reducing depression and anxiety symptoms.
Sleep quality
Regular physical activity improves sleep depth and reduces insomnia, and better sleep improves mood.
Cognitive function
Exercise improves working memory, concentration, and executive function, which is particularly relevant for ADHD.
Self-efficacy
Completing physical challenges, however small, builds a sense of capability that affects other life areas.
Social connection
Group exercise like a team sport, a running club, or a yoga class. All help to fight isolation, a major risk factor for poor mental health.

Youth Mental Health

Young people face unique mental health challenges. Understanding these challenges is the first step toward providing support and intervention.

60%

40%

Social media can significantly impact youth mental health through comparison, cyberbullying, and fear of missing out (FOMO). While it offers connection, excessive use correlates with increased anxiety, depression, and sleep problems.

Healthy habits: Set time limits, curate your feed positively, take regular breaks, and remember that posts show highlight reels, not reality.

Academic stress is one of the leading causes of mental health issues in youth. Pressure to achieve high grades, get into university, and meet expectations can lead to anxiety, burnout, and depression.

Coping strategies: Break tasks into manageable steps, practice self-compassion, seek help when needed, and remember that grades don't define your worth.

Social anxiety, peer pressure, bullying, and fitting in are major concerns. These experiences can lead to school refusal, isolation, and declining mental health.

Support: Talk to a trusted adult, school counselor, or therapist. Building a support network and developing social skills can help navigate these challenges.

As young people develop independence, conflicts with parents and caregivers are common. Communication breakdowns and misunderstandings can create stress and impact mental health.

Tips: Practice open communication, express feelings calmly, listen to understand, and consider family therapy if conflicts persist.

Co-occurring Conditions

Substance Abuse & Mental Health

Substance abuse and mental health disorders often occur together. Nearly 50% of people with severe mental disorders are also affected by substance abuse, and vice versa.

This connection exists because mental health conditions may lead people to self-medicate with drugs or alcohol, while substance use can trigger or worsen mental health symptoms.

Alcohol Withdrawal Warning

Stopping alcohol suddenly can be dangerous if you've been drinking heavily for a long time. Withdrawal symptoms can include:

  • Tremors and shakiness
  • Anxiety and agitation
  • Hallucinations
  • Seizures (in severe cases)
  • Delirium tremens (DTs) - a medical emergency

Always consult a healthcare professional before stopping alcohol. Medically supervised detox may be necessary for your safety.

Supporting Loved Ones

Support for Relatives

Supporting someone with mental health challenges can be difficult. Here are practical tips to help your loved one while taking care of yourself.

01Listen Without Judgment

Create a safe space for them to express feelings. Avoid saying "just cheer up" or "others have it worse." Validate their experience.

02Encourage Professional Help

Gently suggest seeing a mental health professional. Offer to help find a therapist or attend the first appointment with them if they'd like.

03Be Patient

Recovery isn't linear. There will be good days and bad days. Your consistent support makes a difference even when progress seems slow.

04Take Care of Yourself

You can't pour from an empty cup. Set boundaries, seek your own support, and maintain your wellbeing so you can be there for them.

05Learn About Their Condition

Understanding their diagnosis helps you recognize symptoms, triggers, and how to provide appropriate support. Knowledge reduces fear and stigma.

Common questions about mental health services, insurance, and what to expect.

No, you do not need a referral from your GP to see a psychologist. You can book directly with us. However, if you want reimbursement through the public health system, you may need a referral for certain conditions.

Most private health insurance plans in Norway cover psychological services. Coverage varies by provider and plan. We recommend checking with your insurance company before your first session. We can provide documentation for insurance claims.

Psychologists provide therapy and psychological assessments but cannot prescribe medication (in Norway). They focus on talk therapy, cognitive-behavioral interventions, and other therapeutic approaches.

Psychiatrists are medical doctors who can diagnose mental health conditions, prescribe medication, and provide therapy. They typically focus on medication management for moderate to severe mental health conditions.

The duration of therapy varies depending on your needs, goals, and the complexity of your concerns. Some people benefit from short-term therapy (8-12 sessions), while others engage in longer-term work. We'll discuss a treatment plan during your first session and regularly review your progress.

Yes, therapy is confidential. Psychologists are bound by professional ethics and Norwegian law to protect your privacy. Information is only shared without your consent in rare situations involving imminent risk of harm to yourself or others, child abuse, or court orders.
